Network & Cybersecurity

The infrastructure behind "the cloud," and the policy and process that keep it secure. This coursework pulled back the curtain on how modern data centers are designed, built, and kept running — and on how organizations build security programs, write the policies that govern them, and align to established frameworks.

NETECH-113 · Data Center

How data centers really work

Studied the core components of a facility — servers, storage arrays, and network gear — plus the infrastructure that supports them: power distribution units, UPS systems, generators, cooling, and physical security.

  • Design considerations like hot-aisle/cold-aisle layouts, airflow containment, raised floors, and power usage effectiveness (PUE).
  • Operations: inventory control, environmental monitoring (temperature, humidity, power draw), and maintenance workflows.
  • Redundancy and high availability — backup power, network failover, and how virtualization and containerization pack many services onto shared hardware.
NETECH-211 · Linux+

Linux+ certification prep

Prepared for the CompTIA Linux+ certification exam: system management, security, scripting and automation, and troubleshooting on the Linux command line.

I also built LearnLinux+, a study app with a simulated shell, organized around the Linux+ XK0-005 objectives. It's on BEZ Works.

CYBR-111 · Framework

NIST 800-53 deep dive

Wrote an introduction to NIST Special Publication 800-53 (Revision 5) — the framework of security and privacy controls used across U.S. federal agencies and, increasingly, the private sector. It focuses on what outcomes to achieve rather than dictating how, organizing controls into 20 families like Access Control, Incident Response, and Configuration Management. Revision 5's big shift was merging security and privacy controls into one unified catalog.

CYBR-111 · Policy writing

Acceptable Use Policy — "Avengers Campus HQ"

Wrote a full Acceptable Use Policy, using a playful "Avengers Campus HQ" theme to cover the real structure of an AUP: purpose, scope, acceptable and prohibited use, and network/communication rules. (Yes — it prohibits x-ray vision in the locker rooms and using super-powers to bypass security controls.) A fun way to learn how real organizations define appropriate use of systems and facilities.
