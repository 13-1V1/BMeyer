G-Code Trainer

A Windows app that teaches Fanuc mill G-code: lessons and quizzes, a 2D and 3D simulator, part challenges, a control panel, and student profiles.

Download installer → Browser simulator

The problem

G-code is what a CNC mill runs. On a Fanuc mill, a wrong move is a crash. Students need the lesson and a simulator in the same program before they stand at the machine. The app is aimed at a classroom where several people share the computers.

What I built

G-Code Trainer 0.1.16 is the Windows app. It teaches Fanuc mill G-code to people who have not written it before.

  • Lessons and quizzes, then a coding challenge you solve in the live simulator.
  • A simulator with 2D and 3D toolpath views, including animated playback you can scrub and step through.
  • Part challenges that start from a drawing, a tool table, and stock, and ask for a complete program.
  • A control panel for modes, jogging, touching off, and program prove-out.
  • Student profiles, so progress and saved programs stay separate on a shared PC.

The course is inch-native, in G20, because that is how the shop it was written for works. G21 is the switch to millimeters.

The same shop math shows up in the calculators for taper, speeds and feeds, and surface speed, which run in the browser and download from Projects.

How to try it

Download G-Code.Trainer.Setup.0.1.16.exe (about 104 MB). SmartScreen may warn on this student build. Choose More info, then Run anyway, if you trust the source.

The browser page is a simplified simulator for rapids, straight cuts, and arcs, in inches or millimeters, absolute or incremental. Lessons, the 3D view, the control panel, and student profiles are in the Windows installer.
